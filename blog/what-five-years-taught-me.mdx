So this is the thing I spent years trying to figure out, and I want to walk you through all of it, because when it finally clicked for me, everything changed.

For most of my life, I could get whatever I decided I wanted. I wanted to do well in school, I did well. I wanted a good career, I built one. I wanted to travel the world, and I did that too. So there was always this one thing that genuinely confused me, which was that no matter what I tried, my body would not change. I was healthy. I want to be clear about that, I was never unhealthy, I was fine. I just was not in the kind of shape I wanted to be in, and it did not make sense to me that the one guy who could figure out everything else could not figure this out. It took me a long time to understand why, and this is what I found.

## First I had to be honest about what I was actually chasing

If I am being really honest with myself, underneath almost everything I have ever done, there is one thing driving it, and it is status. I know that word sounds a little ugly when you say it out loud, but hear me out, because I do not think it is a bad thing at all. It is one of the most primal things about us as human beings. For most of our history, where you stood in your group decided almost everything about your life. It decided whether you were safe, whether people looked out for you, whether you had access to things, even who wanted to be around you. Status was not vanity, it was survival. And that has not left us. We are still wired for it, deep down, even now that we are not living in tribes anymore.

In some perfect version of the world, none of this would matter and we would all just exist. But that is not the world we live in, and it is not the way we are built. We care where we stand. We always have. And once I admitted to myself that this is what was really underneath my ambition, a lot of things started to make sense that never made sense before.

Now, the way we actually chase that feeling is by succeeding at whatever the people around us count as success. That is the whole game we are all quietly playing. So if status is the why underneath everything, success is the how. And that is where it gets interesting, because I realised I had never actually understood what success even is.

## Then I realised what success actually is

Because here is the part that took me the longest to see. Success is not a fixed thing. It is completely dependent on the environment you are in.

Think about a monk. In a monastery, success means giving up everything. No money, no possessions, no worldly things at all, and reaching some kind of enlightenment. That is the whole definition of a life well lived in that environment. Now take that exact same person and drop them into a startup in San Francisco, and that definition of success would look insane. Different environment, completely different definition of what winning even means. It is not that one is right and one is wrong. It is that success is always decided by wherever you choose to live. And when I understood that, I finally understood what had happened to me when I moved.

## The definition of success changed under my feet

Back home in India, the definition was simple, and honestly it was narrow. What mattered was your marks, your degree, your college, your career, your CTC. That was it. That was the whole game, and I was good at that game. The bar for how you looked and carried yourself was so low it barely registered. If you did not have a belly, you were basically considered fit, and that was the end of it. So I never once thought about my body, and honestly there was no reason to, because in that environment it was simply not something you were measured on.

Then I moved abroad, and the definition quietly widened without anyone telling me. Out here, yes, people still care about your work and your mind. But they also care, a lot, about how you look. How you carry yourself. Your presence when you walk into a room. The impression you leave before you have said a single word. And that was completely new to me, because for the first time in my life, a part of what decided how I was seen was the one thing I had never worked on.

  ![Scoreboard](/images/scoreboard.png "Scoreboard")
</Frame>

That is really where it started. I was suddenly in a new environment with a new definition of success, still playing by the old one I grew up with.

## And then it just kept getting harder

Once I saw that, the rest of it started to make sense too, because it was not just that the target had moved. It was that everything about my situation made hitting that target harder than it had ever been.

<Frame>
  ![Three Forces](/images/three-forces.png "Three Forces")
</Frame>

The first thing was that the bar was on another planet compared to what I knew. Back home, being healthy meant eating clean and getting in some exercise. Home food, not too much junk, and you were doing your part. But out here, that is just the entry point. Now you actually have to understand your body, know your macros, hit the right macros, hold your calories, hit your protein, run a regimented strength training program, and build real lean muscle. The standard I was being measured against was so far above anything I had grown up with that it is not even the same conversation. So every time I caught myself in the mirror before work, I did not just see a soft belly and skinny arms, I saw how far behind I was from a bar I never even knew existed a few years ago.

The second thing was that I had no room left in my life for it. Back home, so much was handled for me. I never really had to cook, or clean, or manage all the small things, so all of my energy could pour into that one thing that mattered. Then I moved, and suddenly every single thing was on me. My food, my laundry, my dishes, all of it. None of it is big on its own, but it all adds up, and it all pulls from the same reserve of energy and time you have in a day. So right when the world started asking more from my body than it ever had, I actually had less left in the tank to give it.

And the third thing was the one that took me the longest to actually see. The accountability I have for my career was never something I was born with, it was wired into me over years by my upbringing. My parents, my school, my whole culture put so much focus on studies and career that holding a high standard there became second nature. It is just a part of who I am now. But fitness was never wired in that way. It was never part of my upbringing, nobody around me growing up treated it as something that mattered, so I never built that same wiring for it.

And that is the real difference between us and everyone around us out here. For them, fitness is not a chore, it is just a part of their life, because it was wired into them exactly the way career was wired into us. It was in their schools, their culture, their upbringing, for years. They are not more disciplined than you. They simply had this one thing built into them that we never did. So for us it feels like a chore, and we never learned how to hold ourselves accountable to it, because it was never a part of our lives to begin with. So on a hard day, when I skipped the workout or the meal I said I would make, nothing happened. Nobody was there, nobody cared whether I got fit, and I had never built the habit of being the one to hold myself to it. So I just let it go.

And this is the part I really did not want to look at, because I am the independent one, I moved abroad and made it happen on my own. Admitting that I could hold an incredibly high standard in my career but could not do the same with my own body when no one was watching felt like a contradiction I did not want to face. But it was true.

## So here is the loop I kept falling into

When you put all of that together, what actually happens is a trap, and I went around it more times than I can count.

The bar is high, I am sitting well below it, and I can feel it costing me every day. So I get anxious about it, and when I get anxious, I do the only thing I know how to do, which is attack the problem the way I have attacked every other problem in my life. I make an aggressive plan. Big deficit, train hard every single day, cut out all the food I love, go all in. Because going all in is how I have won at everything else.

And here is how it actually plays out, every time. I pour in a huge amount of effort. But a couple of weeks in, two things happen at once. I am right on the edge of burning out, and I am still not seeing the results I hoped I would by now. And when you are that drained and the payoff is not showing up, it gets harder and harder to keep putting in that same level of effort day after day.

Then a bad day comes, or a bad week, and it just tips over the line, and I do not know how to handle it. And once that break happens, restarting is so much harder than anyone thinks, because now I know exactly what I am signing myself up for. I know the suffering waiting for me if I get back on. So I push it. I will start again on Monday. And that Monday does not come for a long, long time. It only comes back around when the pain has built up high enough again, when I start feeling bad enough about myself that I really want to change. And then I throw myself back into the exact same cycle, at the exact same intensity, and it breaks in the exact same way.

That is the loop. And notice it was never a discipline problem. It is not that I could not hold a standard on my own, I do that in my career every single day. It is that this particular way of going about it is designed to break, and every time it broke, I blamed myself and made the next attempt even harder, starting from an even lower place.

  ![The Loop](/images/the-loop.png "The Loop")
</Frame>

That is the loop. And the cruel part is you cannot out-work it, because the harder you push, the faster it spins. I genuinely believe this is why nothing ever stuck for me for years. It was never that I lacked discipline. It was this, the entire time.

## Getting fit is inevitable

If you do nothing, you will probably still get there one day. I actually believe that. You are living in an environment where this stuff matters and where everyone around you cares about it, and over enough time that slowly rubs off on you. You are smart, you clearly figured out how to build a life in a new country, so eventually you would piece the fitness thing together too.

But eventually is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and I know that because I lived the eventually. Eventually is years. And every one of those years is another aggressive plan, another collapse, another hit to how you see yourself, another stretch of walking into rooms carrying a body that is quietly working against you, another trip home where your family points it out and it stings every single time. The environment will fix you the way weather eventually wears down a rock. It works, but it is slow, and you take all the damage on the way.

I did not want to keep paying that. And once I understood everything I just told you, I stopped trying to fix it with more effort, because effort was never the thing I was missing, and I started fixing the actual causes instead.

## How I actually fixed it

The way I think about it now is that when I moved abroad, I was handed a second road to walk. My whole life there was one road, my education and my career, and everyone kept me on it. Then abroad I was quietly handed this second road, my body, except this road turned out to be much longer than I expected, moving abroad made it much harder to walk, and nobody had ever taught me how to walk it in the first place. So of course I kept falling off.

<Frame>
  ![Two Roads](/images/two-roads.png "Two Roads")
</Frame>

And once I saw it that way, fixing it came down to three things, and all three are just the opposite of everything that was working against me.

The first is walking the right road, so it is the shortest one. The road is already long enough, so the last thing you want is to waste months wandering down the wrong path, experimenting on yourself, guessing. You want to know the exact right things to be doing from day one, with none of the guesswork, so the road in front of you is as short as it can possibly be.

The second is making the walk as easy as possible. Because a long road you can actually keep walking beats a short one you keep falling off. That means taking all the thinking off your plate, because you have no room left in your life for another project to manage. It means making your fitness bend around your actual life, so it survives your busy weeks and your travel instead of breaking the moment life gets in the way. And it means making it something you genuinely enjoy, especially your food, because for us food is a love language, and I still remember eating my mom's food on a bad day. You learn how to handle your calories and your protein in a way where you can still eat the things you love, because that is the only version of this you will actually stick to.

The third is giving yourself the accountability you never had. This is the big one, because I was never wired to hold this standard on my own, and neither were you. So instead of waiting years for the environment to slowly build that into you, you borrow it from the start. Someone who holds you to the standard on the days you cannot, who keeps you on the road, until one day holding yourself there just becomes who you are. This was the single thing I had never had in my life, and it changed everything.

That is the whole breakdown. That is what I spent years working out, and it is exactly what my coaching is built around now. Over the last three years I have used this same core to help countless Indians living abroad make fitness an automatic part of their life. It comes down to giving them the clarity, the ease, and the accountability they were never handed, all built around their real life, so their body finally matches everything else they have built.

Because you already know that if your body matched your mind, you would be an absolute weapon. You have known that for a long time. This is how you get there.
