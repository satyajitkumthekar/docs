## The Breakdown

So this is the thing I spent years trying to figure out, and I want to walk you through all of it, because when it finally clicked for me, everything changed.

I spent five years hating my body. For the first three years I looked exactly like the Indian stereotype, with skinny arms and stubborn belly fat. The next two years were worse. I had a cushy remote job based out of Silicon Valley, and I slowly turned into a chubby guy. I was never unhealthy, and I want to be clear about that. I just was not in the shape I wanted to be in.

The part that stung the most is that I was still going to the gym. The effort was there. But every hard day ended the same way, with me eating my way through it, and the food was winning by a much bigger margin than the gym ever could.

One thought made all of it worse instead of better. I really believed I was one of the smartest people in any room I walked into, and my career kept proving me right. So if I was that smart, why could I not solve my own body?

A year and a half ago I finally took that question seriously. I went back to first principles. I cut out the 80 percent of fitness advice that is noise and kept the 20 percent that actually changes a body. No extreme diets. No two hour workouts. No living like a monk. Just the basics, done simply, every week.

Six months later I had a lean, toned body for the first time in my life.

That was just over a year ago. Since then, the same approach has grown my page past 58,000 followers and built a six figure coaching business, with dozens of other Indians now running it 1:1. I am genuinely excited to share it with you here, because I think it is going to change the way we look at fitness.

It took me a long time to work out why nothing had worked for all those years. This is what I found.

## First, what everyone is actually chasing

If I am being really honest, underneath almost everything I have ever done, there is one thing driving it, and it is status. I know that word sounds a little ugly when you say it out loud, but hear me out, because I do not think it is a bad thing at all. It is one of the most primal things about us as human beings. For most of our history, where you stood in your group decided almost everything about your life. It decided whether you were safe, whether people looked out for you, whether you had access to things, even who wanted to be around you. Status was not vanity, it was survival. And that has not left us. We are still wired for it, deep down, even now that we are not living in tribes anymore.

In some perfect version of the world, none of this would matter and we would all just exist. But that is not the world we live in, and it is not the way we are built. We care where we stand. We always have.

Now, the way we actually chase that feeling is by succeeding at whatever the people around us count as success. That is the whole game we are all quietly playing. So if status is the why underneath everything, success is the how. And that is where it gets interesting, because most of us have never actually looked at what success even is.

## Then what success actually is

Because here is the part that took the longest to see. Success is not a fixed thing. It is completely dependent on the environment you are in.

Think about a monk. In a monastery, success means giving up everything. No money, no possessions, no worldly things at all, and reaching some kind of enlightenment. That is the whole definition of a life well lived in that environment. Now take that exact same person and drop them into a startup in San Francisco, and that definition of success would look insane. Different environment, completely different definition of what winning even means. It is not that one is right and one is wrong. It is that success is always decided by wherever you choose to live. And once you see that, what happens to us when we move abroad starts to make complete sense.

## The definition of success changes under your feet

Back home in India, the definition is simple, and honestly it is narrow. What matters is your marks, your degree, your college, your career, your CTC. That is it. That is the whole game, and the people I work with were all very good at that game. The bar for how you look and carry yourself is so low it barely registers. If you do not have a belly, you are basically considered fit, and that is the end of it. So most of us never once thought about our bodies, and there was no real reason to, because in that environment it was simply not something you were measured on.

Then you move abroad, and the definition quietly widens without anyone telling you. Out here, yes, people still care about your work and your mind. But they also care, a lot, about how you look. How you carry yourself. Your presence when you walk into a room. The impression you leave before you have said a single word. And that is completely new, because for the first time, a part of what decides how you are seen is the one thing you have never worked on.

That is really where it starts. You are suddenly in a new environment with a new definition of success, still playing by the old one you grew up with.

## And then it just keeps getting harder

Once you see that, the rest of it starts to make sense too, because it is not just that the target has moved. It is that everything about your situation makes hitting that target harder than it has ever been.

<Frame>
  ![Three Forces](/images/three-forces.png "Three Forces")
</Frame>

The first thing is that the bar is on another planet compared to what you knew. Back home, being healthy meant eating clean and getting in some exercise. Home food, not too much junk, and you were doing your part. But out here, that is just the entry point. Now you actually have to understand your body, know your macros, hit the right macros, hold your calories, hit your protein, run a regimented strength training program, and build real lean muscle. The standard you are being measured against is so far above anything you grew up with that it is not even the same conversation. So every time you catch yourself in the mirror before work, you do not just see a soft belly and skinny arms. You see how far behind you are from a bar you never even knew existed a few years ago.

The second thing is that there is no room left in your life for it. Back home, so much was handled for you. You never really had to cook, or clean, or manage all the small things, so all of your energy could pour into the one thing that mattered. Then you move, and suddenly every single thing is on you. Your food, your laundry, your dishes, all of it. None of it is big on its own, but it all adds up, and it all pulls from the same reserve of energy and time you have in a day. So right when the world starts asking more from your body than it ever has, you have less left in the tank to give it.

And the third thing is the one that takes longest to see. The accountability you have for your career was never something you were born with. It was wired into you over years by your upbringing. Your parents, your school, your whole culture put so much focus on studies and career that holding a high standard there became second nature. It is just a part of who you are now. But fitness was never wired in that way. It was never part of your upbringing, nobody around you growing up treated it as something that mattered, so you never built that same wiring for it.

And that is the real difference between us and everyone around us out here. For them, fitness is not a chore, it is just a part of their life, because it was wired into them exactly the way career was wired into us. It was in their schools, their culture, their upbringing, for years. They are not more disciplined than you. They simply had this one thing built into them that we never did. So for us it feels like a chore, and we never learned how to hold ourselves accountable to it, because it was never a part of our lives to begin with. So on a hard day, when you skip the workout or the meal you said you would make, nothing happens. Nobody is there, nobody cares whether you get fit, and you never built the habit of being the one to hold yourself to it. So you let it go.

And this is the part almost nobody wants to look at, because you are the independent one. You moved abroad and made it happen on your own. Admitting that you can hold an incredibly high standard in your career but cannot do the same with your own body when no one is watching feels like a contradiction you would rather not face. But it is true.

## So here is the loop

When you put all of that together, what actually happens is a trap, and I have watched people go around it more times than they can count.

The bar is high, you are sitting well below it, and you can feel it costing you every day. So you get anxious about it, and when you get anxious, you do the only thing you know how to do, which is attack the problem the way you have attacked every other problem in your life. You make an aggressive plan. Big deficit, train hard every single day, cut out all the food you love, go all in. Because going all in is how you have won at everything else.

And here is how it actually plays out, every time. You pour in a huge amount of effort. But a couple of weeks in, two things happen at once. You are right on the edge of burning out, and you are still not seeing the results you hoped you would by now. And when you are that drained and the payoff is not showing up, it gets harder and harder to keep putting in that same level of effort day after day.

Then a bad day comes, or a bad week, and it just tips over the line. And once that break happens, restarting is so much harder than anyone thinks, because now you know exactly what you are signing yourself up for. You know the suffering waiting for you if you get back on. So you push it. You will start again on Monday. And that Monday does not come for a long, long time. It only comes back around when the pain has built up high enough again, when you start feeling bad enough about yourself that you really want to change. And then you throw yourself back into the exact same cycle, at the exact same intensity, and it breaks in the exact same way.

That is the loop. And notice it was never a discipline problem. It is not that you cannot hold a standard on your own, you do that in your career every single day. It is that this particular way of going about it is designed to break, and every time it breaks, you blame yourself and make the next attempt even harder, starting from an even lower place.

And the cruel part is you cannot out-work it, because the harder you push, the faster it spins. This is the single biggest reason nothing has ever stuck. It was never that you lacked discipline. It was this, the entire time.

## You would get there eventually. That is the trap

If you do nothing, you will probably still get there one day. I actually believe that. You are living in an environment where this stuff matters and where everyone around you cares about it, and over enough time that slowly rubs off on you. You are smart, you clearly figured out how to build a life in a new country, so eventually you would piece the fitness thing together too.

But eventually is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Eventually is years. And every one of those years is another aggressive plan, another collapse, another hit to how you see yourself, another stretch of walking into rooms carrying a body that is quietly working against you, another trip home where your family points it out and it stings every single time. The environment will fix you the way weather eventually wears down a rock. It works, but it is slow, and you take all the damage on the way.

That is the price nobody should have to keep paying. And once you understand everything above, you stop trying to fix it with more effort, because effort was never the thing missing, and you start fixing the actual causes instead.

## How we actually fix it

The way I think about it now is that when you move abroad, you get handed a second road to walk. Your whole life there was one road, your education and your career, and everyone kept you on it. Then abroad you are quietly handed this second road, your body, except this road turns out to be much longer than you expected, moving abroad made it much harder to walk, and nobody ever taught you how to walk it in the first place. So of course you keep falling off.

<Frame>
  ![Two Roads](/images/two-roads.png "Two Roads")
</Frame>

And once you see it that way, fixing it comes down to three things, and all three are just the opposite of everything that is working against you.

The first is walking the right road, so it is the shortest one. The road is already long enough, so the last thing you want is to waste months wandering down the wrong path, experimenting on yourself, guessing. You want to know the exact right things to be doing from day one, with none of the guesswork, so the road in front of you is as short as it can possibly be.

The second is making the walk as easy as possible. Because a long road you can actually keep walking beats a short one you keep falling off. That means taking all the thinking off your plate, because you have no room left in your life for another project to manage. It means making your fitness bend around your actual life, so it survives your busy weeks and your travel instead of breaking the moment life gets in the way. And it means making it something you genuinely enjoy, especially your food, because for us food is a love language, and I still remember eating my mom's food on a bad day. You learn how to handle your calories and your protein in a way where you can still eat the things you love, because that is the only version of this you will actually stick to.

The third is giving yourself the accountability you never had. This is the big one, because you were never wired to hold this standard on your own. So instead of waiting years for the environment to slowly build that into you, you borrow it from the start. Someone who holds you to the standard on the days you cannot, who keeps you on the road, until one day holding yourself there just becomes who you are. This is the one thing almost nobody has ever had, and it is the piece that changes everything.

That is the whole breakdown. It is what we were able to identify after working through this one on one with Indians living in the US, Canada, the UK, Europe and Australia, and it is exactly what my coaching is built around now. It is also the reason the program works as well as it does. It comes down to giving people the clarity, the ease, and the accountability they were never handed, all built around their real life, so their body finally matches everything else they have built.

Because you already know that if your body matched your mind, you would be an absolute weapon. You have known that for a long time. This is how you get there.
