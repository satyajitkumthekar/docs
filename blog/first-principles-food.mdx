## The ocean you have been swimming in

When you decided to take your body seriously, you did what you do with every problem. You researched.

You did not find a shortage of information. You found an ocean of it that contradicts itself. Bulk first or cut first. High carb or low carb. No carbs at night. Six small meals. One big meal. Sugar is the enemy, until it is seed oils.

Your intelligence made it worse. You are used to solving problems by researching harder, so you researched more, found more contradictions, and switched approaches more often. You have spent more mental energy deciding what to eat than you have ever spent eating well.

<Frame>
  ![The Ocean Of Advice](/images/the-ocean.png "The Ocean Of Advice")
</Frame>

The real problem is not that all of it is wrong. Some of it is right. The problem is that none of it gives you a way to judge the next thing you read. So every new article puts you back at zero.

## Why the plan was always going to break

At some point you stopped researching and bought an answer instead. A meal plan. A program. A coach who sent a PDF. Chicken, rice and broccoli, weighed out, repeated.

It held for two weeks, maybe three. Then a week arrived that did not look like the plan. A work trip. A wedding. A stretch where you got home at ten every night. And the whole thing collapsed, because a plan is a complete answer to one perfect week, and your life is not one perfect week.

<Frame>
  ![A Plan Vs A Principle](/images/plan-vs-principle.png "A Plan Vs A Principle")
</Frame>

Here is the part nobody gives you credit for. You saw this yourself. You looked at that meal plan and you already knew you would be back at square one the day you stopped eating exactly that food. You were right. That was not you making excuses. That was you correctly diagnosing the product.

## Why the tactics never worked

So you reached for something simpler. Cut the sugar. Add two eggs in the morning. Walk after dinner.

Half of that thinking was correct. You chose things simple enough to survive a real week. But you chose which things because they sounded healthy, and sounding healthy is the only way anyone ever taught you to choose.

A tactic is a fragment broken off a principle, with the principle thrown away.

Two eggs is aimed at protein, which is the right thing. But two eggs is around twelve grams, against a number closer to one hundred and seventy five. Cutting sugar is aimed at the energy balance, which is also the right thing. But it only does something if it happens to drop you below what you burn, and you never measured whether it did.

<Frame>
  ![A Tactic Is A Fragment](/images/the-fragment.png "A Tactic Is A Fragment")
</Frame>

So a tactic works by accident or not at all. When it works, you credit the sugar instead of the deficit and learn the wrong lesson. When it does not, you decide your body is different and learn nothing. Either way the mechanism stays hidden, because the tactic was built with the mechanism removed.

That is why you are still doing some of these things while privately unsure they do anything. You are running the thing you care about on hope.

## The one principle that decides your body

A first principle is what is left when you cannot break something down any further. My test is simple. Has it changed in the last hundred years, and will it change for you.

Food has exactly one.

**Fat is lost when energy out exceeds energy in.**

Eat less than your body burns and you lose. Eat more and you gain. Eat the same and you stay. There is no fourth possibility, and no food is exempt. Not the healthy food, not the food from home, not the salad, and not the dessert. Food is the in. Your training and your daily movement are part of the out.

<Frame>
  ![The Only Principle](/images/the-three-cases.png "The Only Principle")
</Frame>

I know how anticlimactic that feels after years of complexity. But look at what it hands you. Every confident voice in the ocean is now answerable. Keto works when it puts you in a deficit. Fasting works when it puts you in a deficit. Neither one is magic and neither one is required. You finally have a filter, and the ocean stops costing you anything the moment you have a filter for it.

## Protein, the second non negotiable

There is one more input I treat as non negotiable, and only one. Protein.

Calories decide whether the scale moves. Protein decides whether that movement is fat or muscle. When you eat below what you burn, your body goes looking for something to spend, and protein along with training is what convinces it to spend fat and keep muscle. Two people can lose the same ten kilos and end up looking completely different. One of them got smaller. The other one got lean.

<Frame>
  ![The Same Ten Kilos](/images/the-same-ten-kilos.png "The Same Ten Kilos")
</Frame>

So food has two non negotiables, your calories and your protein. Everything else can bend. That is not a loophole to make this easier to sell. Only two things are load bearing, so only two things are protected, and that is exactly why you get to keep the food you love.

## Your two numbers

A principle is not something you can eat. So you turn it into two numbers for your body.

**Your calorie number.** Take your weight in kilograms and multiply by 2.2 to get pounds. Then multiply that by a number for your goal. Ten to twelve if you are losing fat. Thirteen to fifteen if you are maintaining. Sixteen to eighteen if you are gaining muscle. Start with the middle of your range.

An eighty kilogram man who wants to gain muscle does this. Eighty times 2.2 is one hundred and seventy six pounds. One hundred and seventy six times seventeen is roughly three thousand calories a day.

**Your protein number.** Your weight in pounds, in grams. One hundred and seventy six pounds means one hundred and seventy five grams a day. There is no second step and no complicated formula.

<Frame>
  ![Your Two Numbers](/images/your-numbers.png "Your Two Numbers")
</Frame>

If you would rather skip the math, here are the starting numbers using the middle multiplier for each goal.

| Your weight | Losing fat | Maintaining | Gaining muscle | Protein per day |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 60 kg | 1,450 cal | 1,850 cal | 2,250 cal | 130 g |
| 70 kg | 1,700 cal | 2,150 cal | 2,600 cal | 155 g |
| 80 kg | 1,950 cal | 2,450 cal | 3,000 cal | 175 g |
| 90 kg | 2,200 cal | 2,750 cal | 3,350 cal | 200 g |

**This number is an estimate and it is supposed to be.** Nobody's true maintenance is knowable on paper. You use it as a starting point, run it for two weeks without changing anything, and let the result tell you whether it was too high or too low.

**How you know it is working.** Weigh yourself at the same time on the same days and take the weekly average. Never react to a single morning, because daily weight moves with water, salt and sleep, none of which is fat. After two weeks, one of three things is true. If the average is moving the way you want, roughly a quarter to one percent of your bodyweight a week, leave the number alone. If it has not moved and you actually hit your numbers, adjust calories by around two hundred in the direction you need and run another two weeks. If it has not moved and you know you missed days, do not touch the number. Run it properly first.

That is the whole feedback loop. Two weeks from now you will know whether it is working instead of hoping.

**How to track it.** Pick one of these, not all three. MyFitnessPal is the lowest friction option and the free version is more than enough. If you hate apps, tell ChatGPT what you ate as you go and let it keep the running total. If you want the accurate version, use a gram scale, because one banana can be a hundred grams or two hundred and the app cannot tell the difference.

**Then treat the number like money.** Your calories are the budget. Your protein is the fixed cost and you buy it first, every single day. Whatever is left is yours to spend on anything you want. The biryani, the dessert, the drinks with the team. Anything.

<Frame>
  ![Buy Protein First](/images/the-budget.png "Buy Protein First")
</Frame>

That last part is not a concession. It is the design, and it is the reason this survives past week three when everything else did not.

## The methods you will meet

Your number is a fact about your body. It does not tell you how to hit it on a Thursday. That is what a method is for. A method is a repeatable way of landing on your number inside your actual life.

You will meet a lot of them. Here are the ones worth knowing, because I use most of them myself.

**The rotation.** Four or five meals you already like, repeated, so the day stops asking you questions. It needs a kitchen and some free evenings.

**Volume eating.** Food with water in it that takes up room for very little cost. Vegetables, soup, salad, fruit. It is not about health. It is about arriving at dinner not desperate, because desperate is what actually breaks the number.

**The liquid fallback.** A shake is not a health food and it is not cheating. It is thirty grams of protein in thirty seconds on a day that already went wrong.

**Fasting windows.** Sixteen eight, one meal a day. It works if your morning is not yours and you never wanted breakfast anyway.

**Counting the drinks.** For most people who are stuck, alcohol is the largest untracked line in the week. You do not have to stop. You have to count it and then decide whether it is worth what it costs.

<Frame>
  ![The Methods You Will Meet](/images/the-method-catalogue.png "The Methods You Will Meet")
</Frame>

All of these work. But notice what they have in common. Every one of them was designed around a situation, and every one of them is pointed at the deficit. They are tools for getting the number down. None of them was designed around you.

### A word on the fasting windows, because this is where the ocean is loudest

Intermittent fasting, one meal a day, sixteen eight. None of them burn fat by themselves. They are methods, not principles. Every one of them works only when it lands you under your number, and every one of them fails when it does not.

What they actually do is remove decisions. If your mornings are chaotic and you never wanted breakfast, skipping it deletes a meal you had to think about and leaves more of your budget for the meals you care about. That is a real advantage and it is the only one being claimed here.

<Frame>
  ![Same Number, Different Windows](/images/the-windows.png "Same Number, Different Windows")
</Frame>

One warning on the tight windows. One meal a day makes your calorie number easy and your protein number very hard. Getting a hundred and seventy five grams into a single sitting is a lot of food, and most people who try it quietly miss their protein for months and then wonder why they got smaller instead of leaner. If you want a compressed window, run two meals rather than one.

### How to judge any method you are handed

Three tests, and this includes everything above.

**Does it hit the number.** If you cannot show the arithmetic, it is a tactic wearing a method's clothes.

**Does it survive your worst week.** Build for the Thursday you get home at ten with nothing in the fridge, not the Sunday you have three free hours.

**Would you do it for a year.** A method you hate is a countdown to the day you stop.

<Frame>
  ![Does It Pass](/images/the-method-test.png "Does It Pass")
</Frame>

Most methods pass the first test. Some pass the second. Almost none pass the third, because almost none of them were built with your enjoyment as the point.

## The anchor method

After running this with enough people, in enough countries, through enough weeks that did not go to plan, we ended up with one method that we now build with every single person we work with.

Let me be precise about the claim, because I have just spent half this page telling you that everything works when it lands the number, and I am not about to become one of the voices in the ocean.

It is not better at burning fat. Nothing is better at burning fat than anything else once the number is the same.

It is better because it is the only one built for the person instead of the deficit. It asks the least of you, it hands back the most freedom, and it survives more weeks than anything else I have run. It is the only method on this page whose actual purpose is to make the principle enjoyable to live with, which is the reason it passes the third test when almost nothing else does.

And it is not a combination of the methods above. It falls straight out of your two numbers.

Your protein is a fixed cost and it is due every single day, so it needs something that secures it daily. That is the anchor meal.

Your calories are a budget and your body settles that budget across the week rather than the day, so they need something that allocates across the week. That is calorie banking.

<Frame>
  ![The Anchor Method](/images/the-anchor-method.png "The Anchor Method")
</Frame>

Two numbers. Two mechanisms. One method.

### Part one, the anchor meal

One genuinely high protein meal a day that carries most of the load.

Mine sits around seventy grams. That means the rest of the day only has to find another hundred grams across whatever I feel like eating, which happens almost by accident, instead of chasing a hundred and seventy five grams across a day I have to think about constantly.

<Frame>
  ![The Anchor Meal](/images/the-anchor.png "The Anchor Meal")
</Frame>

Build two or three versions of it so it survives a week where you cannot cook. Mine are deliberately a little boring and I have eaten the same four versions for two years, and that is the trade. One meal is repetitive, and every other meal of my day is completely free.

The anchor moves. It can be lunch on a normal day, dinner on a travel day, or the meal at your parents' house on a Sunday. What does not move is that it happens.

### Part two, calorie banking

Your number is written as a daily figure, but your body settles the account over the week.

Say your number is two thousand. Your week is fourteen thousand. A wedding on Saturday that costs you four thousand does not break anything. It costs you sixteen hundred and fifty on the other six days, and the week still lands exactly where it should.

<Frame>
  ![Calorie Banking](/images/calorie-banking.png "Calorie Banking")
</Frame>

Two rules make this work rather than backfire. Bank before the event, never after, because deciding to earn it back next week is how people end up chasing a deficit they never catch. And do not lend so hard on the earlier days that you arrive starving, because a day at twelve hundred to buy Saturday usually ends up costing you Wednesday.

### What the anchor method actually hands you

Fluidity, and this is the whole point of it.

Because your protein is secured by one meal and your calories are allocated across the week, the shape of your day is genuinely free. One meal on a travel day, because there was one window and it was dinner. Two on a normal office day. Five when you are home and grazing. Any of those is fine, and none of them is a rule you have to follow tomorrow.

<Frame>
  ![The Shape Is Yours](/images/three-shapes.png "The Shape Is Yours")
</Frame>

Read the day rather than obey a plan. Three questions do it, and after a few weeks you stop noticing you are asking them.

How many windows do I actually have today, which tells you how many meals there are. Which meal today is not mine to control, which tells you where the anchor goes and what everything else has to shrink around. And is this shape carrying my protein, which tells you whether the fallback needs to appear.

Every other method on this page forces a shape on your day. This is the only one that hands the shape back to you.

### The situations you already know are coming

These are the defaults you decide once, in advance, so the situation does not get to decide for you.

| The situation | What you do |
| :-- | :-- |
| Restaurant | The anchor is that meal. Pick the protein first and build the order around it. |
| Travel day | Assume one real meal and one fallback. Anchor at the airport, not on the plane. |
| Family food at home | Eat the food. Add a protein of your own to the plate and let the carbs be what they are. |
| A week with no time to cook | Anchor plus fallback. Nothing that needs a free evening. |
| A wedding or a big night | Bank across the days before. Hold your protein on the day and spend everything else. |
| Drinking | Count it in advance, eat your anchor before you go, accept the cost. |
| A trip back to India | Maintenance, not a deficit. Anchor daily, walk daily, and enjoy your mother's cooking. |
| A week that fell apart | Do not restart on Monday. Hit your anchor tomorrow and the week is back. |

### When it breaks

It is a stressed Thursday, you are tired, and the anchor does not get made.

You have not failed the principle. The principle asks nothing of you. You have not failed your number in any permanent way either. You failed one meal, on one evening. You reach for the fallback, or you carry it into tomorrow, and the week still lands.

That is why this does not collapse the way a plan does. There is nothing rigid in it to snap.

## Building it for your life

Two decisions and you are running it.

**Pick your anchors.** Two or three versions of one high protein meal that you would happily eat every week for a year. One that you cook, one that you can buy near your office or your home, and one that requires nothing at all. Check the protein on each of them once, properly, and then never think about it again.

**Find the spend in your week.** Look at the next seven days and find the meal that is not yours to control. The dinner, the party, the family lunch. That is what you bank for, and the banking starts from the first day of the week rather than the day before.

Then one question, and it is the one that actually decides whether this holds. Write down the specific moment that broke you last time. Not the story, the moment. The evening you got home at ten with nothing in the fridge. The client dinner. Friday night after the week you had.

That moment is your constraint, and your anchor has to be built to beat it. If your constraint is late evenings, your anchor is lunch. If your constraint is a chaotic morning, your anchor is dinner and your morning is a fallback. You are not building a system. You are removing the one or two moments that keep breaking you.

## The stack

So here is the whole thing, small enough to hold in your head.

At the bottom is the principle, handed to you once, never changing again. On top of it is your number, which is your clarity. On top of that is your method, which is your ease. And on top of everything is fluency.

<Frame>
  ![The Stack](/images/the-food-stack.png "The Stack")
</Frame>

Nothing above the principle is sacred. When your life changes, and it will, you do not start over. You go back to the principle, produce a new number and new anchors for the new life, and carry on. The plan had nothing underneath it, so when it broke there was nothing to rebuild from. This has a foundation, so a break is a repair job instead of a restart.

You also start learning from every week. For years you have had attempts without data, because you never knew why anything went the way it went. Now every week answers something. The scale did not move, so the deficit was not real, so which meals were bigger than I thought. Ten attempts stop being ten resets and become ten lessons.

## Fluency, the part you are actually watching

Have you ever watched someone in great shape move through a brutal week and wondered where the discipline comes from?

Here is a real Tuesday. You are travelling, you slept badly, you have a client dinner at eight, and you have not trained in three days. That day does not arrive as a food question with a food answer. Answering it means spending across everything at once. Hold your protein and let the carbs go tonight. Eat light through the day because dinner is the day's spend. Take the steps, because the gym is not happening tomorrow either.

<Frame>
  ![A Real Tuesday](/images/a-real-tuesday.png "A Real Tuesday")
</Frame>

The person you are watching is not being disciplined. He is running an allocation he has run a thousand times. That is fluency, and it is the last thing to arrive. You use the principle enough times, in enough situations, and one day it is simply how you think. Once it is how you think, no situation is new. Not the wedding, not the trip home, not the quarter from hell.

That is what this was always going to take. Not a stricter plan. A principle used until it became yours.

## How I run this myself

Here is what the principle looks like after it has been lived in for a while. It settles into a small set of rules, and these are mine.

I treat every day as a budget. Calories are the budget, protein is the fixed cost I pay first, and whatever is left is mine to spend on whatever I want, with no guilt attached to any of it.

I do not eat when I am not hungry. Every bite eaten without wanting it is budget spent on nothing, taken from the version of me a few hours later who will actually be craving something.

I know my trade. Between feeling lean, feeling full, and having every craving satisfied, you get to pick two on any given day. I am never willing to give up lean, so every meal is only ever a choice between the other two. Knowing that in advance ends the negotiation before it starts.

At any meal around other people, I make the trade consciously. Sometimes the food, the company and the moment are worth more than what I give up, and I eat with no guilt at all. But it is always a decision I made, never something that happened to me.

And I give myself no free passes. Short of being stranded in the Himalayas or the Sahara, no situation excuses me. If I can only stay on track in the right environment, I am a slave to that environment, and I lose everything the day it changes.
