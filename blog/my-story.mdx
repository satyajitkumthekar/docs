People ask me how a product guy from India ended up in Bali, coaching Indians all over the world on their bodies. This is the full story.

## I got lucky as a kid

I grew up as a competitive swimmer. I swam at the national level through the second half of school. I won state gold medals. I was sportsman of the year four years in a row.

So fitness was a big part of my childhood, and I want to be honest about that, because most Indians never get it. I did not earn it. I was lucky. Somebody woke me up early. Somebody drove me to the pool. Somebody built the schedule and told me what to do in it. Training every day was not a decision I made. It was just what my life looked like.

I did not know how rare that was until much later. And I did not understand the real problem with it until later still. All of that structure came from outside me. So when school ended and the swimming ended with it, the structure went too, and I had nothing of my own to replace it.

## I became average

Everything swimming had given me disappeared at once. The medals stopped meaning anything the day I stopped competing. The body I had, I had because somebody else was making me train.

Then I got into BITS Pilani, and suddenly I was surrounded by people who were sharper than me, funnier than me, and better read than me. They had backgrounds I could not match. I was not the athlete in the room anymore. For the first time in my life, I was average, and I had no idea what to do with that.

So I tried to fix it sideways. I picked up basketball and spent two years trying to get good enough to matter at it. I went to the gym and put on some size. I spent my time around louder, more confident people, because I could not be that on my own, so I borrowed it from them.

None of it worked. I was still exactly the same person underneath. All I had done was move the problem around.

## The year my mind turned on me

Then 2020 happened.

I was stuck at home. Everything that used to tell me who I was had been taken away at the same time. My mind had always had something to point itself at, a race, a season, a target. Now it had nothing.

So it started pointing at me.

I want to be clear about what that actually means, because "I struggled with my mental health" is a sentence people read and forget. Thoughts started coming faster than I could handle them. Bad thoughts about myself, all day, on a loop I could not turn off. Anxious thoughts about my body. Thoughts that went against everything I had ever believed about who I was. And I could not argue with any of them, because arguing was the thing I had always been good at, and it stopped working.

I coped the way a lot of people cope. I got hooked on online games. I would play for hours, ride the high, and then crash. By Tuesday afternoon I would be lying in bed feeling flat and bored and empty, waiting for the evening so I could do it again. That was most of my week, for months.

But the worst part was not any single thought. It was what sat underneath all of them. My mind was the one thing I had trusted my entire life. It won me everything I had ever won. It was how I saw myself. And now it was not on my side. The one tool I relied on had become the thing I needed protection from.

And nobody knew. Not one person around me could see any of it. I had dinner with my family every single night through the worst of it and they had no idea. If you had asked anyone who knew me that year how I was doing, they would have told you I was fine.

That has stayed with me more than almost anything else, and it is a big reason I coach the way I do now. The people I work with are high performers. From the outside, their lives look sorted. What is actually happening inside them is invisible to everyone, and quite often to them.

## The work nobody saw

I did not go to therapy. I wanted to find my own way through it first. That was my choice at the time, and I would not give it to anyone else as advice.

What I did instead was start meditating. My mother had been doing it for years and she was the one who pointed me to it. I started with Sadhguru's videos, and I watched some of them fifty or sixty times. Not because I was studying them. Because for those twenty minutes the noise in my head got quieter, and I wanted that back. Then I signed up for his Inner Engineering course and started an actual daily practice.

For the first three months, nothing happened. I sat there every day and felt like I was wasting my time. Then something quietly changed.

After that I meditated every single day for more than two years. Not retreats. Not weekends. Every day. It was slow, it was boring, and there was nothing to show anyone for it.

Two things came out of those years, and both of them changed my life. The first is that I learned my thoughts are not me. A scary thought is just a thought I have not looked at properly yet. Once you can see it that way, it loses most of its power over you. The second is that I learned to put a gap between what shows up in my head and what I let myself become. That gap is about half a second wide, and it is the difference between reacting to your life and running it.

While everyone around me chased the next thing, I mostly stayed back. I did not go after the big titles or the loudest opportunities. I had one project, which was fixing what was broken inside me, and it was the only real progress I was making. I was not going to split my attention.

There is nothing to photograph from those years. That is exactly why nobody tells this part of their story. It is also the reason everything after it worked.

If you have ever wondered where the reflection work in my coaching comes from, this is where it comes from. I did not learn it from a certification. I did it on myself first, when it was the only thing I had.

## Winning on paper, flinching in the mirror

Once the big fight was won, the smaller ones showed up.

I was not happy with how I looked. I had acne. I was getting softer. And I had quietly stopped trying. I grew a beard, not because I thought it looked better, but because I had decided that looking after myself was not worth the effort if the body underneath was already a lost cause. You do not even notice yourself giving up like that. It happens as one small decision at a time.

It never showed up as one big painful moment either. It showed up in tiny ones. The half second of hesitation before walking into a room full of new people. Catching myself putting good looking people on a pedestal without ever deciding to. The one look in the mirror after a shower that I would try to forget about ten seconds later. Sitting down and feeling my belly fold over itself, then quietly shifting how I sat so it was less obvious.

None of those things are big enough to complain about. That is exactly why they do so much damage. It is death by a thousand cuts. Nobody around you can see a single one of them, you tell yourself none of them matter, and then you look up and five years have gone.

Meanwhile my career took off. I got one of the highest paying jobs out of my batch, and then a product role at a Silicon Valley startup, fully remote, right in the middle of the AI boom. The company went from zero to a unicorn in under 18 months. I was in rooms with genuinely brilliant people and I was holding my own.

On paper I was winning at everything. In the mirror I still flinched. And every hard day still ended with food, because food was the one reliable thing that made the day feel better for twenty minutes.

That is the exact life most of the people I work with are living on the day they first message me. Winning on the outside, flinching in private. I was running the same script they are running now.

## The body, finally

Five years of hating my body. And the whole time, I was still going to the gym. That is the part people miss. It was not a discipline problem. The effort was there. It just never added up to anything, because I had no idea which parts of it mattered, and every hard day quietly undid the work anyway.

The question that finally broke it open was a simple one. If I am as smart as I think I am, why can I not solve this?

So I stopped taking advice and went back to first principles. I looked at everything I had ever been told about fitness and asked which of it actually decides whether a body changes. Most of it does not. I cut out the 80 percent that is noise, kept the 20 percent that actually does the work, and then I did that small part properly, every week. No extreme diets. No two hour workouts. No living like a monk. No giving up the food I actually like.

Six months later, the man in the mirror finally matched the man I had been claiming to be since school.

It only went that fast for one reason. The fight inside me was already over. I had won it years earlier, in those quiet years nobody saw. Most people try to change their body while they are still fighting a war inside that they have never even named. That is why it takes them years, and that is why they keep breaking and starting again.

I got the order right. Mind first. Body second. The body turned out to be the easy part.

## The problems I have now are different ones

I started writing about all of this online. In about a year the page grew past 58,000 followers, and the coaching I had picked up on the side went from nothing to a six figure business.

At some point it stopped making any sense to keep it as a side project. The method was working too well, on too many people, for me to give it anything less than everything I had. So I quit the job, packed my bags, and moved to Bali, which is about as close to a Silicon Valley of fitness as this planet has. I am writing this from here.

Here is the honest part. Reading back what I just wrote, I struggle to recognise the person in it. Not in a dramatic way, and not because I have forgotten it. I just cannot get back into that version of me anymore. I do not know what it feels like to dread a mirror. I do not know what it is like to end a bad day inside a fridge. Those things did not get easier to manage. They stopped being things.

I am not telling you my life has no problems. It has plenty. They are simply much bigger and much better problems, and not one of them is about my body, or about my own head being somewhere I did not want to be. That is what doing this work properly actually buys you. You do not manage the old problems for the rest of your life. You finish them, and then you get to spend everything you have on the things that actually matter to you.

I could not be more excited to put all of it in front of you. Everything I worked out is written up in these posts, and all of it is free.
