People ask how a product guy from India ended up in Bali, coaching Indians around the world on their bodies. This is the long version, told straight.

## The luck I was born into

I grew up a competitive swimmer. National level through the second half of school, state gold medals, sportsman of the year four times in a row. Fitness was a deep part of my growing up, and I want to be honest about that, because most Indians never get it. I did nothing to earn it. I was lucky. I landed in an environment where training every day was simply what life looked like, with a coach, a pool, and a schedule built around me.

That is an unfair advantage, and I did not understand how rare it was until much later. What I understood even later was that the advantage was on loan. A sport handed to you as a kid is borrowed structure, and borrowed structure leaves when the season ends.

## When the structure left

Mine ended, and everything it had given me left with it. I walked into a room full of people sharper than me, with backgrounds I could not match, and for the first time in my life I was average.

I tried to rebuild sideways. Other sports. The gym. Time spent in the orbit of louder, more confident people, because I could not generate my own. None of it filled the gap. It just changed the angle of the gap.

## The year my mind turned on me

Then came 2020. Everything that had ever told me who I was got taken away at the same time, and for the first time in my life my mind had no project to point itself at.

So it pointed at me.

I want to be specific here, because "I struggled with my mental health" is the kind of sentence people nod at and scroll past. Thoughts started arriving faster than I could process them. Negative thoughts about myself, running all day, on a loop I could not switch off. Paranoid thoughts about my body. Thoughts that contradicted every single thing I had ever believed about who I was.

I coped the way people cope. I found things that gave me a hit, rode them for hours, and then crashed so hard that by the middle of the week I would be lying there flat and bored and waiting to do it again.

The worst part was not any single thought. It was what sat underneath all of them. My mind was the one tool I had trusted for every win in my life. It was the entire basis of how I saw myself. And it was no longer on my side. The instrument had turned into the thing I needed protecting from.

And nobody knew. Not one person around me could see any of it. I ate dinner with my family every night through the worst of it, and if you had asked anyone who knew me that year how I was doing, they would have said fine.

That has stayed with me more than almost anything else, and it is a large part of why I coach the way I do now. The people I work with are high performers. From the outside their lives look sorted. What is actually going on inside them is invisible to everyone, and sometimes to them.

## The work nobody saw

I did not go to therapy. I wanted to find my own way through first. That was my call at the time, and I would not hand it to anyone else as advice.

What I did instead was start meditating. My mother had been doing it for years and she pointed me towards it. For the first three months, nothing happened. Then something quietly unlocked.

What followed was more than two years of daily practice. Not retreats. Not weekends. Daily. Slow, boring, internal unknotting with nothing to show for it. Two things came out of those years and both of them changed my life. I learned that my thoughts are not me, that a paranoid thought is just a thought that has not been observed yet. And I learned to put a gap between what arrives in my head and what I let myself become.

While everyone around me chased the next thing, I mostly held back. I had one project, which was fixing what was broken inside, and it was the only real progress I was making, so I refused to dilute it.

There is nothing to photograph from those years, which is exactly why nobody tells this part of their story. It is also the reason everything after it worked. If you have ever wondered where the reflection work inside my coaching comes from, it comes from here. I did not learn it from a certification. I lived it first, on myself, when it was the only thing I had.

## Winning on paper, flinching in the mirror

Once the big fight was won, the smaller ones surfaced. I was not happy with how I looked. I had acne, I was getting softer, and I had quietly stopped trying. I grew a beard, not to look better, but because I had decided grooming was not worth the effort if the canvas underneath was already lost. It is the kind of small surrender you do not even notice yourself making.

Meanwhile the career took off. A product role at a Silicon Valley startup, fully remote, right in the middle of the AI boom, at a company that went from zero to a unicorn in under 18 months. On paper I was winning at everything. In the mirror I still flinched, and every rough day still ended with food.

That contradiction, surface success with a private flinch, is the exact life most of the people I work with are living on the day they first message me. I was running the same script they are running.

## The body, finally

Five years of hating my body, and the question that finally cracked it was this. If I am as smart as I think I am, why can I not solve this?

So I went back to first principles. I cut the 80 percent of fitness advice that is noise, kept the 20 percent that actually changes a body, and executed it simply, every week. No extreme diets. No two hour routines. No living like a monk.

Six months later, the man in the mirror finally matched the man I had been claiming to be since school.

It went that fast for one reason. The internal war was already over, won years earlier in the quiet years nobody saw. Most people try to change their body while still fighting a war inside they have never named, which is why it takes them years and breaks them over and over. I had the order right. Mind first, body second. The body turned out to be the easy part.

## Still in the middle

I started writing about all of it, and people started asking me to coach them. That became my whole life faster than I expected. I moved to Bali, and I am writing this from there.

I am not writing this from the end of a journey. I am visibly in the middle of one, still training, still reflecting, still running the same system I hand the people I work with, and honestly that is the only position I trust anyone to teach from.

Every part of what I coach against, I lived first. The single dimension life. The surface success with the private flinch. The years of knowing exactly what needed to happen and watching myself not do it. And every part of the fix existed in my life before it ever existed as a method.

If any of this rhymes with your own story, everything I worked out is written up across these posts, and all of it is free.
