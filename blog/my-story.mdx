People ask me how a product guy from India ended up in Bali, coaching Indians around the world on their bodies. The short version lives on [the framework page](/blog/what-five-years-taught-me). This is the long one, told straight, in the order it actually happened.

## The swimmer

I grew up a competitive swimmer. National level through the second half of school, state gold medals, sportsman of the year four times in a row. Fitness was a deep part of my growing up, and I want to be honest about that, because most Indians never get it. I did nothing to earn that start. I was lucky. I happened to land in an environment where training every day was simply what life looked like, with a coach, a pool, and a schedule built around me. That is an unfair advantage, and I only understood years later how rare it was. What I did not understand back then was that the advantage was on loan. A sport handed to you as a kid is borrowed structure, and borrowed structure leaves when the season ends. I was also the kid who was elite at exactly one thing and clumsy at almost everything else, and school did not let me forget it. I thought being elite in one dimension meant I had a life. I had results. I did not have a life.

## Average for the first time

College ended swimming, and everything it gave me left with it. I walked into BITS Pilani, into a campus full of people smarter than me with backgrounds I could not match, and for the first time in my life I was average. I tried to rebuild sideways. Basketball for two years. The gym. Borrowing the orbit of louder, more popular friends because I could not generate my own. None of it filled the gap. It just changed the angle of the gap.

## The year my mind turned on me

Then came 2020. Second year of college, locked down at home, and everything that had ever told me who I was got taken away at the same time. Swimming was already gone. The campus was gone. The new version of me had not been built yet. For the first time in my life, my mind had no project to point itself at.

So it pointed at me.

I want to be specific here, because "I struggled with my mental health" is the kind of sentence people nod at and scroll past. What actually happened is that thoughts started arriving faster than I could process them. Negative thoughts about myself, running all day, on a loop I could not switch off. Paranoid thoughts about my body. Thoughts that contradicted every single thing I had ever believed about who I was.

There was a stretch where I could not shake the fear that I would not be able to tell a dream from real life. It started after watching Inception, of all things, and it did not stay a movie thought. It sat on me for weeks. I would be sitting in my own house, in the room I grew up in, quietly checking whether any of it was actually happening.

I got addicted to online gaming, and I mean addicted. I would ride a dopamine peak for hours and then crash so hard that by Tuesday afternoon I would be lying there bored and flatly depressed, waiting for the next hit. A stray dog sniffed me one afternoon, so I went home and read about rabies, and then I read more, and then I could not stop, and I spent weeks inside a paranoia loop I could not argue my way out of. I knew the odds. Knowing the odds did nothing.

The worst part was not any single thought. It was what sat underneath all of them. My mind was the one tool I had trusted for every win in my life. It got me the medals. It got me into BITS. It was the entire basis of how I saw myself. And it was no longer on my side. The instrument had turned into the thing I needed protecting from.

And nobody knew. Not one person around me could see any of it. I ate dinner with my family every night through the worst of it. If you had asked anyone who knew me that year how I was doing, they would have said fine. That has stayed with me more than almost anything else, and it is a large part of why I coach the way I do now. The people I work with are high performers. From the outside their lives look sorted. What is actually going on inside them is invisible to everyone, and sometimes to them.

## The work nobody saw

I did not go to therapy. I want to be honest about that instead of dressing it up as a philosophy. At the time I felt I understood myself better than a stranger could, and I wanted to find my own way through first. That was my call, made at 20, and I would not hand it to anyone else as advice.

What I did instead was start meditating. My mother had been doing it for years, and she pointed me to Sadhguru. I watched some of those videos fifty or sixty times, not because I was studying them but because something in them put the noise down for a while. I signed up for his Inner Engineering course and started a daily practice. For the first three months, nothing. Then something quietly unlocked.

What followed was more than two years of daily meditation. Not retreats. Not weekends. Daily, through the rest of college. Slow, boring, internal unknotting with nothing to show for it. What I got out of it was two things that changed my entire life. I learned that my thoughts are not me, that a paranoid thought is just a thought that has not been observed yet. And I learned to put a gap between what arrives in my head and what I let myself become.

While my batchmates chased positions and internships, I mostly held back. I had one project, which was fixing what was broken inside, and it was the only real progress I was making, so I refused to dilute it.

There is nothing to photograph from those years, which is exactly why nobody tells this part of their story. It is also the reason everything after it worked. If you have ever wondered where the reflection work inside my coaching comes from, it comes from here. I did not learn it from a certification. I lived it first, on myself, when it was the only thing I had.

## Winning on paper, flinching in the mirror

By the final year of college the big fight was won, so the smaller ones surfaced. I was not happy with how I looked. I had acne, I was getting softer, and I had quietly stopped trying. I grew a beard, not to look better, but because I had decided grooming was not worth the effort if the canvas underneath was already lost. It is the kind of small surrender you do not even notice yourself making. Meanwhile the career took off. One of the highest paying internships in my batch, Gurugram, then Bangalore, then a product role at a Silicon Valley startup, fully remote, right in the middle of the AI boom, at a company that went from zero to a unicorn in under 18 months. On paper I was winning at everything. In the mirror I still flinched. Every rough day still ended with food. That contradiction, surface success with an internal flinch, is the exact life most of my clients are living on the day they first message me. I was running the same script they are running. I just did not have anyone to hand me the answer.

## The body, finally

You already know this part if you have read [the framework page](/blog/what-five-years-taught-me). I finally took the question seriously, the one that had been sitting there for years. If I was that smart, why could I not solve my own body? I went back to first principles, cut the 80 percent of fitness advice that is noise, kept the 20 percent that actually changes a body, and executed the core simply, every week. Six months later, the man in the mirror finally matched the man I had been claiming to be since school. And it went that fast for one reason. The internal war was already over, won years earlier in those quiet meditation years. Most people try to transform their body while still fighting a war inside they have not even named, which is why it takes them years and breaks them over and over. I had the order right, mind first, body second, and the body turned out to be the easy part.

## The leap

Right after, I started the Instagram page, because I had always known I was not built for the corporate ladder, and doing a job that was not mine had felt like wearing someone else's clothes for years. Three months into the page, I took a leap I could not really afford. I hired a business coach with money I did not have a backup for, and I started coaching. The business became my whole life faster than I expected. I moved to Bali, and I am writing this from there.

## Still in the middle

I am not writing this from the end of a journey. I am visibly in the middle of it, still training, still reflecting, still running the same system I hand my clients, and honestly that is the only position I trust anyone to teach from. Every part of the disease I coach against, I lived first. The single dimension life. The surface success with the internal flinch. The years of knowing exactly what needed to happen and watching myself not do it. And every part of the fix existed in my life before it ever existed as a method.
