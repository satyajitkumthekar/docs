People ask me how a product guy from India ended up in Bali, coaching Indians all over the world on their bodies. This is the full story.

## I got lucky as a kid

I grew up as a competitive swimmer. I swam at the national level through the second half of school. I won state gold medals. I was sportsman of the year four years in a row.

So fitness was a big part of my childhood, and I want to be honest about that, because most Indians never get it. I did not earn it. I was lucky. I grew up around people for whom training every day was just what life looked like. I had a coach. I had a pool. I had a schedule that somebody else built for me.

I did not know how rare that was until much later. And I did not understand the real problem with it until later still. All of that structure came from outside me. So when the swimming ended, the structure went with it, and I had nothing of my own to replace it.

## I became average

Everything swimming gave me disappeared at once.

Suddenly I was in a room full of people who were sharper than me, with backgrounds I could not match. For the first time in my life, I was average.

I tried to fix it sideways. I played other sports. I went to the gym. I spent my time around louder, more confident people, because I could not be that on my own. None of it worked. I still felt exactly the same.

## The year my mind turned on me

Then 2020 happened.

Everything that used to tell me who I was had been taken away. My mind had always had something to work on. Now it had nothing.

So it started working on me.

I want to be clear about what that actually means, because "I struggled with my mental health" is a sentence people read and forget. Thoughts started coming faster than I could handle them. Bad thoughts about myself, all day, on a loop I could not turn off. Anxious thoughts about my body. Thoughts that went against everything I had ever believed about who I was.

I coped the way most people cope. I found things that gave me a quick high, I did them for hours, and then I crashed. By the middle of the week I would be lying there feeling flat and empty, waiting to do it all again.

But the worst part was not any single thought. It was what sat underneath all of them. My mind was the one thing I had trusted my whole life. It won me everything I had ever won. It was how I saw myself. And now it was not on my side. The one tool I relied on had become the thing I needed protection from.

And nobody knew. Not one person around me could see any of it. I had dinner with my family every night through the worst of it. If you had asked anyone who knew me that year how I was doing, they would have told you I was fine.

That has stayed with me more than almost anything else, and it is a big reason I coach the way I do now. The people I work with are high performers. From the outside, their lives look sorted. What is actually happening inside them is invisible to everyone, and sometimes even to them.

## The work nobody saw

I did not go to therapy. I wanted to find my own way through it first. That was my choice at the time, and I would not give it to anyone else as advice.

What I did instead was start meditating. My mother had been doing it for years and she pointed me to it. For the first three months, nothing happened. Then something quietly changed.

After that I meditated every single day for more than two years. Not retreats. Not weekends. Every day. It was slow and it was boring and there was nothing to show for it.

Two things came out of those years, and both of them changed my life. The first is that I learned my thoughts are not me. A scary thought is just a thought I have not looked at properly yet. The second is that I learned to put a gap between what shows up in my head and what I let myself become.

While everyone around me chased the next thing, I mostly stayed back. I had one project, which was fixing what was broken inside me. It was the only real progress I was making, so I did not want to split my attention.

There is nothing to photograph from those years. That is exactly why nobody tells this part of their story. It is also the reason everything after it worked.

If you have ever wondered where the reflection work in my coaching comes from, it comes from here. I did not learn it from a certification. I did it on myself first, when it was the only thing I had.

## Winning on paper, flinching in the mirror

Once the big fight was won, the smaller ones showed up.

I was not happy with how I looked. I had acne. I was getting softer. And I had quietly stopped trying. I grew a beard, not because I thought it looked better, but because I had decided that looking after myself was not worth the effort if the body underneath was already a lost cause. You do not even notice yourself giving up like that.

Meanwhile my career took off. I got a product role at a Silicon Valley startup, fully remote, right in the middle of the AI boom. The company went from zero to a unicorn in under 18 months.

On paper I was winning at everything. In the mirror I still flinched. And every hard day still ended with food.

That is the exact life most of the people I work with are living on the day they first message me. Successful on the outside, unhappy in private. I was running the same script they are running now.

## The body, finally

Five years of hating my body. The question that finally broke it open was a simple one. If I am as smart as I think I am, why can I not solve this?

So I went back to first principles. I cut out the 80 percent of fitness advice that is noise. I kept the 20 percent that actually changes a body. Then I did it simply, every week. No extreme diets. No two hour workouts. No living like a monk.

Six months later, the man in the mirror finally matched the man I had been claiming to be since school.

It only went that fast for one reason. The fight inside me was already over. I had won it years earlier, in those quiet years nobody saw. Most people try to change their body while they are still fighting a war inside that they have never even named. That is why it takes them years, and why they keep breaking.

I got the order right. Mind first. Body second. The body turned out to be the easy part.

## I am still in the middle of it

I started writing about all of this online, and people started asking me to coach them. It became my whole life faster than I expected. I moved to Bali, and I am writing this from here.

I am not writing this from the end of the journey. I am in the middle of it. I still train. I still reflect. I still run the same system I give the people I work with. Honestly, that is the only place I would trust anyone to teach from.

Everything I coach against, I lived first. The life that only works in one area. The success on the outside with the flinch in private. The years of knowing exactly what I had to do and watching myself not do it.

And everything I use to fix it existed in my life before it was ever a method.
