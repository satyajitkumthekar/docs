You have a very specific image of what a fitness routine is supposed to look like.

Six in the morning, in the gym. Six days a week. Ninety minute sessions. The perfect diet running alongside it. Every week, exactly the same.

Social media drilled it in. Movies reinforced it. And now that image sits in your head as the definition of what a real routine looks like.

And because your actual life never matches that picture, you tell yourself it is not the right time.

So you wait. You wait for the calm season, for the project to wrap, for the travel to slow down, for life to settle. That window where everything aligns barely ever comes, and when it does it lasts two weeks before something else takes over.

Meanwhile the person who stopped waiting and started adapting is three years in.

## Part one. You are not failing a routine

You are failing an image of a routine, which is a different thing.

<Frame>
  ![The picture in your head, and the thing that works](/images/walk/picture-vs-reality.png "The picture in your head, and the thing that works")
</Frame>

This is not about lowering the bar. It is about being honest about what your actual life requires and then building something that survives it.

My own training has not looked the same once in three years. Weeks of travel. A startup scaling from zero to a unicorn. Moving cities. Family. The routine changed constantly. The commitment never did.

<Note>
  **The shift.** Stop trying to build a routine that survives your ideal life. Build one that survives your actual life.
</Note>

The difference between people who stay in shape for years and people who cycle in and out is not discipline, and it is not willpower, and it is not even motivation. It is the ability to recognise which season they are in and match the approach to that season instead of forcing a mismatch.

## Part two. Find your season

Look at your life honestly. Not the version you want, the version you are actually living this week.

<Frame>
  ![Find your season, then match your approach to it](/images/walk/four-seasons.png "Find your season, then match your approach to it")
</Frame>

|  | Super busy | Chaos | Moderate | Light |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **When you are in it** | Travel, crisis, deadlines. No gym access. Zero predictability. | 60 plus hour weeks. Unpredictable calendar. Barely time to think. | 40 to 50 hour weeks. Mostly predictable. Manageable chaos. | Light schedule. Sleep dialled in. Energy high. Life aligned. |
| **Your training** | 30 min home session every 2 days | 2 full body gym sessions a week, any 2 days | 4 upper and lower sessions a week, any order | 6 push, pull and legs sessions a week, twice through |
| **Your nutrition** | Hit protein. Move as much as possible. | Hit protein. Hit 8,000 steps. Everything else free. | Anchor meal locked in. Calories roughly tracked. | Full tracking. Protein dialled. Fully intentional. |
| **The goal** | Retain muscle. Keep the habit alive. | Protect what you have built. | Maintain. Steady progress. | Grow. Maximum adaptation. |

Your season can change week to week. That is the entire point. The routine shifts the moment the season does, rather than two weeks after you have already missed everything and decided you have fallen off.

<Note>
  The Moderate season is the twelve week program from **The Paved Path**. That document is what a normal week looks like. This one is what the other weeks look like.
</Note>

## Part three. Training, by season

### Super busy. The thirty minute home session

No gym, no equipment, no problem. This is the session you do when everything else has collapsed. Travel, deadlines, moving, family. The goal is narrow: retain the muscle you have built and keep the habit alive.

Do this every two days. Three or four sessions a week, each under thirty minutes.

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Push ups, feet elevated if too easy | 3 to failure | Max reps | 60 sec |
| Pike Push ups | 3 to failure | Max reps | 60 sec |
| Bodyweight Squats or Jump Squats | 3 to failure | Max reps | 60 sec |
| Reverse Lunges, each leg | 3 to failure | 12 to 20 each | 60 sec |
| Glute Bridges or Single Leg Glute Bridge | 3 to failure | Max reps | 45 sec |
| Diamond Push ups | 2 to failure | Max reps | 60 sec |
| Plank Hold | 2 | Max time | 45 sec |

Every set still goes to failure. Short on time does not mean short on effort.

<Tip>
  **If you have a resistance band,** add band pull aparts and banded rows at the end. Ten extra minutes and a large difference for your back and rear delts on travel weeks.
</Tip>

This session exists for one reason. To keep you in the game when everything else has fallen apart. It does not need to be perfect. It needs to happen.

### Chaos. Two full body sessions

Any two days that week. You are not building here, you are protecting. Two sessions a week is enough to hold onto almost everything you have built, and it costs you ninety minutes total across seven days.

### Moderate. The four day split

This is the program in The Paved Path, and it is where actual progress gets made. Most of your year should look like this.

### Light. Six sessions, push pull legs, twice through

Your growth window. These seasons are rarer than you think, so when one arrives, use it properly rather than treating it as normal and feeling like a failure the rest of the year.

## Part four. Making it require no thinking

The reason most routines die is not the training. It is the number of decisions the routine asks you to make on days when you have none left.

So you make all of them once, on Sunday.

<Frame>
  ![The Sunday ten minutes](/images/walk/sunday-ritual.png "The Sunday ten minutes")
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Be honest about the week">
    Look at what is actually on the calendar, not what you hope it will be. Which season are you in?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the weekly target">
    Three sessions if it is chaos. Four if it is moderate. Six if everything is aligned.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find your gaps and block them">
    Look at the week, find your three to six openings, mornings or lunches or evenings, it genuinely does not matter which, and block them like CEO meetings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forget about it until the block hits">
    No daily decisions. No daily negotiation with yourself. You show up and execute.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**The anchor workout.** Pick one session that happens no matter how bad the week gets. Mine was always Saturday morning. Build the chaos around it rather than the other way around, because a week where everything collapsed except one session is still a week you did not fall off.

The goal here is zero daily decisions about your fitness. You made all of them on Sunday. Now you just execute.

## Part five. Food you would eat anyway

This is the part most programs get wrong, and it is the part that decides whether you are still doing this in a year.

For us, food is a love language. It is how our families showed up for us. I still remember eating my mother's food on a bad day and what that did. Any plan that treats food purely as fuel, that strips out everything you actually love eating, is a plan you will follow for five weeks and then resent.

So the approach is not restriction. It is making one part of your day do all the work, so the rest of it can be whatever you want.

### The anchor meal

Instead of trying to optimise every meal, you fix one meal a day that does all the heavy lifting. Your protein, your nutrients, everything your body actually needs. Lock that in and the rest of your day is free.

<Frame>
  ![The anchor meal](/images/walk/anchor-meal.png "The anchor meal")
</Frame>

Every anchor meal starts from the same base. Two scoops of whey, which is 250 calories and 50 grams of protein.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Take your daily calorie target">
    From The Paved Path, your bodyweight in pounds multiplied by the number for your goal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Multiply it by 0.40">
    That is your anchor meal budget.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spend the remaining sixty percent however you want">
    No tracking. No stress. No guilt. This is the part that makes the whole thing survivable.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Then pick your anchor meal from the tier that matches your budget.

| Budget | Non veg | Protein | Veg | Protein |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 500 to 600 cal | 2 scoops whey, 150g grilled chicken, big salad | 80g | 2 scoops whey in 300g Greek yoghurt, 200g low fat cottage cheese, berries | 95g |
| 700 to 800 cal | 2 scoops whey, 200g grilled chicken, 2 whole eggs, salad | 105g | 2 scoops whey in 300g Greek yoghurt, 200g low fat paneer, salad | 110g |
| 900 to 1000 cal | 2 scoops whey in milk, 300g chicken, 3 whole eggs, salad | 140g | 2 scoops whey in milk, 400g firm tofu, 300g Greek yoghurt, salad | 120g |

Notice what this does. Once the anchor meal is done, most people have already hit their protein target for the day before lunch. Everything after that is genuinely free.

### Nutrition by season

Same principle as training. Stop trying to eat perfectly every day and match the effort to the season you are in.

| Season | What you actually do |
| :-- | :-- |
| Chaos | Two non negotiables. Hit your protein target and hit 8,000 steps. Everything else is free and you do not stress about any of it. |
| Moderate | Anchor meal locked in. One or two high protein meals doing the heavy lifting daily. The rest of your calories are yours. |
| Light | Fully intentional. Calories tracked, protein dialled, every meal considered. This is your growth window, so make it count. |

On your hardest weeks the goal is not to eat perfectly. The goal is to not undo everything you have built. Protein and steps keep your muscle, keep your metabolism, and keep the habit alive. On a chaos week that is enough, and treating it as enough is what stops the spiral.

## Part six. The only thing that actually breaks a routine

<Frame>
  ![What actually breaks a routine](/images/walk/it-all-counts.png "What actually breaks a routine")
</Frame>

It is not travel. It is not a busy week. It is not a lack of time or equipment or the perfect plan.

The only thing that breaks a routine is the belief that anything less than the ideal version does not count.

Three sessions in a chaos week counts. Hitting protein on a travel day counts. A thirty minute home workout when you had planned a ninety minute session counts. It all counts, it all adds up, and the people who understand that are the ones still going three years later while everyone else is waiting for the right time.

The routine adapts. The commitment does not. That is the skill.

## The honest part

You now have the map from The Paved Path and the flexibility to actually walk it. Between them, that is clarity and ease, which are two of the three things that were missing.

And I will be straight with you again, because there is still a gap here that this document cannot close.

Everything above assumes you will do it. It assumes that on Sunday you will sit down for ten minutes and be honest about your week. It assumes that when the block hits on Wednesday evening and you are tired and nobody would ever know, you go anyway.

That is not information. That is accountability, and it is the one thing you were never wired for, because fitness was never built into your upbringing the way your career was. The people around you out here are not more disciplined than you. They simply had this one thing installed in them for years, and you did not.

That is the third part, and it is the one that decides everything.
